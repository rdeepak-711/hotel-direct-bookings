2.1 How X ranks posts in October 2026

X open-sourced a new For You algorithm on 20 Jan 2026 [S6] and keeps the repo at github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm. The README was last updated 18 Sep 2026 and the weights file is synced from production values [S1] [S2]. I read the README, home-mixer/params/param.rs, home-mixer/scorers/author_cold_start.rs and docs/BIDIRECTIONAL_BOOST_CHANGE.md. sourced

How it works: a model predicts how likely each viewer is to take each action on a post, and the score is the sum of weight × predicted probability. The README warns that weights multiply probabilities, not counts. So a weight of 5 doesn't mean one reply equals five likes. It means the model's guess that you will reply moves the score more. sourced

Five rules in the code matter for a 33-follower account: sourced

  • Small authors get a trial slot, briefly. The cold-start scorer gives a slot around position 15–16 in a viewer's feed to posts that are original (not a reply, not a repost), from authors with at most 50,000 followers, with fewer than 200 impressions, and less than 2 hours old. Among the eligible posts, it samples using each post's likes per home-feed view [S3] [S2].
  • Posts expire after 48 hours. AgeFilter removes posts older than 48 hours from For You [S1].
  • Your own posts compete with each other. "Each post after an author's first is multiplied by a decaying factor, down to a floor" (Author Diversity) [S1].
  • Strangers' feeds discount you. Posts from accounts the viewer doesn't follow are multiplied by a factor below 1, and so are replies and reposts from accounts they do follow [S1].
  • Links are not penalised in the code. "Open link" carries a small positive weight (0.2) [S2]. On 28–29 July 2026, X's product head Nikita Bier told Mark Zuckerberg "you do not need to put the links in replies anymore", and Elon Musk replied to Paul Graham "We haven't for over a year" [S5]. An October 2024 test by Jesse Colombo found 94% fewer views on a linked post [S6], but that predates both the new code and Musk's statement.

What that means for you inference

  • The first two hours of an original post decide most of its reach, and the trial is judged on likes per view. Post when your audience is awake, and be around to answer replies.
  • Four posts a day split the same small pool of viewers, and each one after the first is discounted. Fewer, better posts.
  • Profile clicks no longer score anything. A profile visit only matters if the profile turns it into a follow, a link click or a DM.
  • Mutual follows are the strongest lever the code gives a small account. Thirty mutuals who actually reply are worth more than three hundred passive followers.
  • The "link in the first reply" rule can relax. Put the link in the post when the click is the point (a demo, the repo). Keep discussion posts link-free because they're meant to get replies.

2.4 Audits as content, and their ethics

  • Public roasting builds reach, not necessarily money. AyeshaBuilds roasted landing pages in public for free before building RoastMyLanding. TrustMRR shows $48 total revenue across three products, 3 paying customers and 430 followers [S21]. The public examples I found were founders who submitted their own URLs or tagged the roaster. Nobody was roasted uninvited. sourced
  • Paid roasts are sold as private. A search summary describes Olly's Roast My Landing Page as "100% private" for paid roasts, claiming £70,000 of work from 200 roasts. The page redirected to another domain, so I could not open it. unverified
  • Google's rules make public quoting of real reviews hard anyway. Google's Places policies say "You must always credit the author when displaying photos or reviews", that end users "must always have access to view the individual source… review on Google Maps", and that Places content can't be cached apart from place IDs [S22]. sourced A screenshot of a real review in a post would need the author's name and a link to the review, which identifies the hotel. inference
  • Anonymising is weaker than it looks. A search summary of an Australian law firm's note says a business can be defamed without being named if it is identifiable from the description. I couldn't open the page (HTTP 503). unverified The practical point stands either way: "a 120-room hotel on the Portland waterfront" names the hotel. inference

Policy for this project inference, consistent with the standing rule in docs/public-build-playbook.md

  1. Fixture data ("Harborside Inn", invented reviews) for every screenshot of a report, labelled "fixture data".
  2. Aggregates only for real data: "12 hotels in one US city, 60 reviews", counts, no hotel details, no review text.
  3. Praise only if a real hotel is ever named: something it does well, with no score next to it.
  4. Consent, privately for a full report on a real hotel: only for the hotel's own owner or staff, and never posted.
  5. Never name Hotelzify publicly. The spec says the public site carries no Hotelzify name, and that applies to posts too.

2.5 Small-account specifics

  • 0 to 500 in 30 days, mostly from replies (self-reported, HackerNoon, Apr 2026). The author credits about 80% of growth to replies under large accounts. He wrote 15–20 quality replies a day for two weeks, picked 10 niche accounts with 5K–100K followers, and judged results after 30 days. What failed: AI-generated replies ("essentially flat"), mass-following, and generic tips threads ("drove almost no follows"). His own caveat: the percentages are estimates [S23]. sourced
  • Timing. Sprout Social (2 billion engagements, 307,000 profiles, Nov 2025–Feb 2026): best overall Tue–Thu 12–6 p.m. in the audience's local time. For Software & Technology: Tue–Thu 11 a.m.–4 p.m., and avoid Saturday [S24]. sourced
  • Frequency. Buffer suggests 3–5 posts a day, but the source is "a study of 30 top X accounts" [S25]. sourced That advice is for large accounts. For an account getting 4 views a post, it means more posts splitting the same tiny audience. inference
  • Communities. I found no measured data on reach for small accounts posting in X Communities. Only marketing blogs, which I'm not relying on. no evidence
  • Bio, banner and pinned post. No controlled study exists. The benchmarks floating around (10% visit-to-follow and similar) come from blogs without data. no evidence Four of the people in 2.2 (levelsio, marclou, tdinh_me, yongfook) put product names and monthly revenue straight in the bio [S8]. sourced

3. Patterns that convert

"Convert" here means a profile click that becomes a follow, a link click, a sign-up or a DM, not just views.

Show the output

The biggest breakouts from small or new accounts showed the product doing its job: Chatbase's demo video, HeadshotPro's AI photos (2.07M views) sourced. For you, the output is the report, and a report with dropped quotes visible is more interesting than one without. inference

Numbers with their context

levelsio's milestone posts carry customers, price, costs and next steps, not just a total sourced. You have no revenue, so use numbers that are true: tests, reviews checked, quotes dropped, hotels in a sample. inference

Honest small numbers and failures

"Not great, but it's a start" (175K), "4-figure biz 🤣" (22K), "Daily fluctuations… are brutal" (28K) sourced. Your "I killed my first idea with 60 reviews" is this type. inference

Invisible engineering, for the hiring audience

Arvid's "a massive change… that no one will ever see" got about twice the views of his revenue post sourced. Engineering posts reach engineers and hiring managers, not buyers. inference

Public commitments

#QuitInPublic (Damon Chen) and #applyinpublic (KP) both ended in outcomes sourced. A dated public goal ("v1 live by [date]") gives people a reason to come back. inference

Replies before posts

Highest code weight among common actions, plus the mutual-follow boost, plus the only self-reported case sourced. Replies say something specific or disagree with a reason. No link drops in other people's threads. inference

Links where the click is the point

X says no link penalty, and the code weights link opens positively sourced. Keep "?ref=x" on every link so clicks can be counted on your side. inference

Open revenue is optional

The open-startup pioneer now says sharing revenue rarely helps sourced. It doesn't apply yet, since there's no revenue. Note it for later. inference

What doesn't convert, per the sources: generic tips threads, AI-written replies, mass-following [S23]. sourced I'd add teasers before something exists, and "Day N" posts with no result in them. inference (findings.md already notes no controlled evidence on teasers.)

Target 10–20 accounts with 1K–50K followers you'll see repeatedly. These are the future mutuals. inference

9. What to stop doing

  • Four posts a day while posts get 2–5 views. More posts split the same small audience and each one is discounted [S1]. Two a day until views recover.
  • Anything scripted or in bursts. The drop in section 1 starts the day after the 16 Oct burst. Use X's own scheduler, at a normal pace, and nothing else.
  • Repeated identical self-replies ("Code: … Live packets: …" posted more than once). One link reply per post, worded for that post.
  • Five topics a week. LifeDots, northern lights and Claude Code setup tips don't build toward the AI-engineer hire or the hotel-tech audience. Two lanes until 30 Nov: AI verification engineering and Direct Booking Check. Other projects get one mention each, in the pinned hub. inference
  • Task IDs as hooks. "ENG-010" and "step 1 of 13" belong at the end of a post, not the start. Lead with what happened.
  • Generic tips posts ("my Claude Code setup: 3 models, 3 jobs"). The only source that measured them found good impressions but "almost no follows" [S23].
  • Treating views or followers as the goal. Count conversations with hiring people and hotel-tech people instead (section 10).
  • Real hotel names, real review text, or Hotelzify's name in public. Covered in 2.4.
